The autonomous aerial capture of non-cooperative targets by Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) poses critical control and perception challenges caused by visual detection noise, sampling delays, temporary detection losses, and severe ground-effect disturbances. To overcome these limitations, in the context of retrieving entomological traps for the Vector Tracking System (VTS) project, this work develops and validates a closed-loop Position-Based Visual Servoing (PBVS) system using a single camera. The proposed architecture structures the approach into three sequential operational phases, integrating lightweight convolutional neural models from the YOLO family (YOLO26n and YOLOv10n) specialized for each optical regime, achieving an average precision exceeding 99.2\% (mAP@50) with inference rates from 28.6 to 51.8 FPS. To mitigate noise from the YOLO-generated bounding boxes, an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) acts as a kinematic estimator, reducing fluctuations by 52\% to 91\% and ensuring trajectory maintenance for up to 4.0 seconds of total visual occlusion. The 2D coordinates are projected into 3D metric Cartesian space via an analytical pinhole model, feeding a cascaded Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller with EKF-derived velocity to damp oscillations and prevent overshoot. A dual-zone optical hysteresis strategy guarantees trap retention in the field of view 100\% of the time, while the attitude controller converges with a mean yaw error of $0.46^\circ$ and a standard deviation of $\pm 0.54^\circ$. The system was designed to couple a motorized robotic gripper mounted on the underside structure of the UAV. Due to the development schedule, coupling feasibility was validated in the Gazebo Sim physics simulator integrated with ArduPilot SITL, evaluating the alignment of the gripper claws relative to the trap handles within a tolerance window of $\pm 8\text{ mm}$. In a stochastic campaign of 60 Monte Carlo missions with ground truth validation, the system achieved a pre-descent alignment root mean square error (RMSE) of $3.13\text{ mm}$ at an altitude of $0.20\text{ m}$, a terminal ground-contact RMSE of $5.25\text{ mm}$, and a coupling success rate of $76.7\%$, establishing a robust, millimeter-accurate solution for autonomous aerial manipulation without fiducial markers.
